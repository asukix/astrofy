## Creation and destruction
When I am trying to understand the thinking space, I am creating my own mental model. This is quite easy. The hard part for me is the destruction of the model. I found that sometimes the destruction can also be quite easy.

## Difficulties in destruction
I noticed 2 types of difficulties while I was rebuilding my mental model:
1. When I create my mental model from scratch by understanding the problem space.
2. When I used a model that I learnt but it's not really mine.

### Destruction of my really own mental model
When I build my mental model from a problem space, it is so hard for me to destroy it. It is hard because I made it. I spent **a lot of time understanding the problem and creating a solution.** This process makes happiness, and after this I feel: ok, I am done, this looks good, and this is what I made. And that's why I feel this is mine, and that's why the destruction is hard, and that's why I don't want to do it. But if I do it, the new model is usually better because my understanding improved.

#### Destruction example
Let's give a real world example. If I create a Lego house that I imagine, I can build it and I enjoy the building of the house. When it is ready it feels good, but the building was the most enjoyable part. After that I don't want to destroy it. But when I do it and rebuild it again, it will be better, and I also can feel the happiness of the building.

### A built in mental model
A built in model for me is when:
- I learnt something and I used it a lot of time, and
- I used it as a base, and
- I build new things on top of it.
But it is a learnt built in model, not really my own model.

#### Difficulties in destruction of a built in mental model
When I try to understand a built in model's background and its problem space, I try to make my own solution. In this situation I noticed I use my old understanding and just recreate a very similar model. And this is the hard part, because I can't forget the solution that I learnt earlier. It is good because I can understand the model better, but it is hard because it is not my own model. That is not a problem — we can use others' models because we don't have time to rebuild everything from scratch. The problem is when I want to create my own mental model from a built in one.

## Make the destruction easier
What I love about the Thinking Space System is that I have time to think, and I have time:
- to understand the problem, and
- to make my own solution.

### Using questions
During this process my most powerful tool is asking questions. I found that when I'm stuck with destruction, questions help me. The interesting thing here: the more questions I ask, the more curiosity it creates, which creates more questions. And this process helps me change my mind. With this, I don't really concentrate on the destruction, even while building. And the building process automatically destroys the old model. I mainly use it with my own mental models, but it also works with built in models.

#### Architecture example
Let's check an architecture example together with MVVM, and let me add very short answers for the example. MVVM is Model-View-ViewModel. *But what is the model?* The model is the place of the business logic. And here are some more questions as examples:
- What is the difference between the model and the model in the view model? — The model in the view model shouldn't contain business logic or calculation.
- How do I connect the model, the view, and the view model? — One connection is that the View contains the VM, and the VM contains the Model.
- Are there other elements in MVVM? — Yes, the converter that can be used by the View.

### Explanation and structured thinking
When I try to rebuild a built in model, it is also hard for me. I noticed something during:
- sharing my thoughts, and
- writing my own articles.
When I try to explain something, it helps me find my gaps. And it also helps me try to understand the problem again, which leads me to rebuild my model.

#### Rebuild your built in model
Have you ever tried to explain something to someone and found that the person didn't understand it? What I did here was try to explain it from a different angle. And this helps me rebuild my understanding too. And this is also an enjoyable way, because I can make my brain work — I give it a problem. And this time the destruction is easier for me.
I usually use this technique for built in models, but it is also good for rebuilding my own mental models.

## Recap

### Learnt model vs built model
I found an important distinction between a learnt model and a model that I built from scratch. If I build my mental model, I can rebuild my solution anytime. So it can be easily changed. This means it can evolve. When I use a learnt model, I can just use it, and I had to remember it. I can't build it. I can't evolve it. And to tell the truth, I feel that **I don't really understand it.** That's why I prefer my own models.

### Make it easier
The destruction of a mental model is not so easy, because I feel it is mine or already built in. The questions raised because of curiosity, or a different approach to the problem, help me make it easier.
